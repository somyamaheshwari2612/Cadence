## Use “human label + exact value”

Don’t hide the actual number completely. Pair a friendly label with the technical value so the app remains transparent.

| Technical field | Primary UI label | Supporting value | Tooltip title |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM2.5 | **Air particles** | `47 µg/m³` | “What are air particles?” |
| PM10 | **Coarse dust** | `82 µg/m³` | “What is coarse dust?” |
| Apparent temperature | **Feels like** | `35°C` | “Why does it feel hotter?” |
| Precipitation probability | **Chance of rain** | `20%` | “What does 20% mean?” |
| Wind speed | **Wind** | `12 km/h · ESE` | “How is wind measured?” |
| Humidity | **Humidity** | `68%` | “Why does humidity matter?” |
| Visibility | **Visibility** | `8 km` | “What affects visibility?” |
| Product ranking | **Outdoor comfort score** | `Lower is better` | “How is this ordered?” |

For the scheduler itself, avoid making “Air particles” sound like a validated health score. Use copy such as:

> **Air particles forecast**  
> 47 µg/m³ PM2.5 · modeled forecast

That is friendlier than leading with `PM2.5`, while remaining honest.

## Suggested recommendation copy

Instead of:

> PM2.5: 47 µg/m³  
> Apparent temperature: 31°C  
> Precipitation probability: 10%

Use:

> **Air particles:** Lower than later today  
> 47 µg/m³ PM2.5  
>
> **Feels like:** 31°C  
>  
> **Chance of rain:** 10%

Then show the information icon after each label:

```text
Air particles  ⓘ
47 µg/m³ PM2.5
```

## Tooltip content

Keep tooltips short—one plain-language explanation, then the technical name.

### Air particles ⓘ

> Tiny particles in the air that can come from traffic, dust, burning, and industry.  
> **Technical measure:** PM2.5, shown in micrograms per cubic metre (µg/m³).  
> This is a modeled forecast for your selected location, not a local monitor reading.

### Feels like ⓘ

> A weather estimate that combines temperature with humidity, wind, and sunlight. It can feel warmer or cooler than the measured air temperature.  
> **Technical measure:** apparent temperature.

### Outdoor comfort score ⓘ

> A Cadence ranking used to compare available time windows using your selected preferences for air particles, feels-like temperature, and rain.  
> It is not a health score, medical assessment, or guarantee of safety.

### Forecast quality ⓘ

> Air-quality values are model estimates for a regional grid, so actual neighborhood conditions may differ. Check local official guidance if conditions are concerning.

## Use Indian terminology carefully

For India, avoid showing **“AQI”** as the main label if the value is Open-Meteo’s `us_aqi`. Users will naturally assume it means CPCB’s familiar Indian AQI.

Use one of these instead:

- **Air particles forecast** — recommended
- **Air quality forecast**
- **Outdoor air conditions**
- **Pollution forecast** — more direct, but can feel alarming
- **Fine-particle level** — accurate, slightly more technical

If you later add a genuine CPCB-derived estimate, you can show:

```text
Air quality category: Moderate ⓘ
Forecast-based CPCB PM estimate — not an official station AQI
```

CPCB uses six color-coded categories specifically to communicate air quality and likely impacts in accessible language, so your UI’s plain-language-first strategy aligns well with how India’s public guidance is designed. [ncdc.mohfw.gov](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/air4.pdf)

## Interaction design

- **Desktop:** hover or keyboard focus on `ⓘ` opens the tooltip.
- **Mobile:** tap opens a small bottom sheet or anchored popover; hover does not exist on touch devices.
- **Accessibility:** Make the icon a real button with an accessible name, e.g. `aria-label="What are air particles?"`; make the popover dismissible via Escape and tap outside.
- **Do not rely only on color:** Pair color states with words such as “Lower than later today,” “Above your preference,” or “Data unavailable.”
- **Keep the tooltip out of the decision path:** Users should understand the main recommendation without opening it.

## Good labels for Cadence

A clean Cadence card could be:

```text
BEST WINDOW FOR YOUR WALK
6:00–6:45 PM · Meets your preferences

Air particles ⓘ        Feels like ⓘ       Rain chance ⓘ
47 µg/m³ PM2.5         29°C               10%

Why this time?
Lower air-particle forecast and cooler feels-like
conditions than most other available windows.
```

That feels approachable for ordinary users without concealing what the application is actually measuring.
